## 2. The thread that matters most right now: current

This opened late and is probably the most important thing in the document,
because it connects to the takeoff bug and to a fire risk.

### The evidence

- Bench supply limited to 2 A. Meter never reads above ~1 A, but **the board
  resets when the motors engage.**
- On a LiPo, a **6 A fuse keeps blowing**.
- At one point a **9 A fuse did not blow and the fuse housing melted.**

### What that actually means

The bench meter is averaging over a few hundred ms. Motor current transients
are milliseconds. It reads ~1 A average while the real demand slams into the
2 A limit, the supply drops into CC, the rail collapses, and the board browns
out. **That reset is the measurement.** The supply is clipping the event and
then showing the average of what it allowed through.

**The melted housing is the key physical evidence, and it refutes the transient
theory.** Plastic has thermal mass — melting a housing takes watts for
*seconds*. So the bot pulls high single-digit amps **continuously**, not in
spikes. The mechanism at the holder is contact resistance, not the fuse
element: ~20 mΩ of loose or oxidised clip at 8 A is 1.3 W into a contact patch
the size of a grain of rice, and it is thermally regenerative. The 9 A element
sat below its threshold the whole time, which is exactly why the holder became
the thing that failed. Going 6 A → 9 A moved the weakest link from the part
designed to fail safely to the part that isn't.

### Why a capacitor does not fix it (worked, do not re-propose)

Motor surge is not a spike. Current rises on the electrical time constant
(L/R, ~0.1–0.5 ms) and then **stays** high until the rotor builds back-EMF —
the *mechanical* time constant, 100s of ms.

    C = I·Δt/ΔV
    15 A for 100 ms within 1 V  →  1.5 FARADS
    20 A for   2 ms within 1 V  →  40,000 µF
    4,700 µF at 20 A            →  235 µs before it sags a volt

A bulk cap is still worth fitting (PWM ripple, bridge-local loop) but it is a
rounding error against the event that opens the fuse. It also makes **connect**
inrush worse from a low-ESR LiPo — if a fuse has ever gone the instant he
plugged in, that is the mechanism and a precharge resistor is the answer.

Placement, if fitted: **load side of the fuse**, at the RoboClaw's main
terminals, short fat leads. A cap upstream of the fuse buys nothing.

### Why it is probably the same event as the takeoff

Ryan: *"If it takes off the motors drive for seconds."* A bot driving into the
floor or a stop is a **locked rotor at full duty, continuously** — precisely
the load that melts a holder. The control bug and the thermal event are likely
one event. See §3.

### What was implemented this session (untested on hardware)

`GETCURRENTS` (49) and `SETM1/M2MAXCURRENT` (133/134) were already in the
opcode enum in `roboclaw.c` and **referenced nowhere else in the tree** — he
had no current instrumentation and no current limit.

- `roboclaw_currents()` — signed int16, 10 mA units. **Signed matters:** a
  braking motor reads negative; decoding unsigned turns −2 A into 653 A.
- `roboclaw_set_max_current()` / `roboclaw_read_max_current()`.
- `motor_hal_read_currents()`, `motor_hal_set_current_limit()`,
  `motor_hal_read_current_limit()` — mutex-guarded, link-down aware.
- Limit pushed in `motor_hal_init()` **before** the encoder reset, so it is
  live before anything can command motion. `motor_config_apply()` pushes it
  too, so a dashboard change reaches hardware, not just the struct.
- `MOTOR_MAX_AMPS_DEFAULT 2.5f` → 5 A total, under a 6 A fuse. Persisted as
  `max_amps` in the `[motor]` section. The IPC handler **rejects** >30 A
  rather than clamping silently.
- Telemetry at 20 Hz with a firmware-side running peak. 20 Hz cannot see a
  1 ms edge and is not trying to — the RoboClaw's own limit handles fast
  events in hardware. What this catches is the **sustained** draw.
- Header ribbon (visible on every tab): `Amps 0.31/0.28  pk 1.85`. Colour is
  judged against the configured limit — red means the controller is clamping
  and the bot is no longer getting the torque it asked for. Click to clear
  peaks (`reset_amp_peaks` IPC command).
- Typed "Current Limit" entry in the Claw card, labelled with the firmware key.

### FIRST THING TO CHECK after the next build

**Confirm `motor_hal_read_current_limit()` reads back 2.5.** Older RoboClaw
firmware does not implement 133/134, and the readback exists specifically to
tell whether the limit is real or silently ignored. If it does not read back,
the hardware limit is a no-op and everything above needs rethinking.

Second: the telemetry packet caps at 4096 bytes and this added ~70.
`ipc_build_and_send()` already detects truncation properly — it logs
`telemetry packet truncated at N bytes, not sent` and drops the packet rather
than sending a fragment. **If the dashboard goes quiet after the sync, check
`botlog` for that line** and trim a field.

### Also unaddressed

`accel_qpps` is **mode 2 only** (`balance_bot.h`, and it is documented there).
The bot runs **mode 1** — plain velocity — so there is **no acceleration ramp
at all**. Every setpoint change is a step and the RoboClaw's velocity PID does
whatever duty it takes to hit it immediately. di/dt is unbounded by design.
Moving to mode 2 with a sane `accel_qpps` is a real lever on this and has not
been tried.

## 3. THE open bug: boot takeoff

**Ryan's deadline, stated 09-17: "I really need it to find its way home every
time within the next day or 2."** This is the priority.

### Symptom

The bot boots **armed but out of bounds** on a kickstand at roughly ±19°. It
should sit still until picked up. Sometimes it instead drives the motors for
seconds, indefinitely, until interrupted. It is always trimmed when this
happens. `arm_at_boot` must keep working — he needs it to balance after the
service starts in case he has to demo without connecting.

### The leading unfalsified lead

**A motor cut in this firmware is an omission, not an action.** Setting
`motor_output_ready = 0` makes the main loop **skip `motor_hal_set_both()`
entirely**. In velocity mode the RoboClaw then holds its last velocity
setpoint forever. Nothing in that path ever commands zero. An OOB gate that
"cuts the motors" therefore does not cut anything — it stops arguing with a
controller that is already driving.

This is why the IMU staleness watchdog added this session **did not fix it**
("it did the exact same thing"). The guard fired; the guard's action was to
stop sending, which changes nothing.

**The fix direction:** an active `motor_hal_coast()` (which sends
`roboclaw_duty_m1m2(0,0)`) **every tick** while the cut condition holds, not a
skipped write. `set_both(0,0)` is NOT sufficient in velocity mode — that is a
closed-loop request to *hold* zero, not an absence of drive.

### Second lead, also unaddressed

Motor init ordering: the e-stop is released before the motors are zeroed in
`motor_hal_init()`. Should be coast → confirm → then deassert e-stop, and
zero the setpoint before asserting e-stop on shutdown.

### The fact that was never obtained — GET THIS FIRST

    cat /etc/default/balance_bot

`balance_bot.service` runs `/usr/local/bin/balance_bot $BOT_ARGS` with
`BOT_ARGS` from that file. **If it contains `-i sbus`, the service boots with
the receiver live and a non-centred stick can command up to `MAX_THETA_REF`
(17°)** — which would drive the motors hard and indefinitely and fits "always
trimmed when this happens" far better than any IMU-convergence story. If it is
`-i none`, SBUS is off the table and the cause is inside `arm_at_boot`.

This single line splits the problem in two and was asked for but never
returned. **Ask for it before theorising.**

## 4. Refuted this session — do not re-litigate

### The dashboard outage

Cause: **Ryan ran `start.sh` himself while experimenting with the VS Code
tasks.** Confirmed by him. `bbot_run START` in the kernel ring buffer has
exactly one emitter — `start.sh` line 118 — and no build task, Makefile target
or `dependsOn` reaches it.

Mechanism: `start.sh` line 132 did `pkill -f serve_web.py`, which SIGTERMs the
**systemd unit's own process**. systemd scores SIGTERM as a clean exit, so
`Restart=on-failure` correctly declined to restart it. `start.sh` then ran its
own copy as a background child and its cleanup trap killed that on exit. Every
run took the dashboard down, brought it back under its own PID, then killed it
for good.

**Fixed** in `start.sh`: it now checks `systemctl is-active`, stops the unit
properly, records that, and restarts it in cleanup.

Wrong theories I produced before checking, all contradicted by something Ryan
had already said — **do not revisit any of these**: the Makefile change, the
`ctrl+shift+b` build, a missing systemd unit, the `bweb` alias, power cycling,
`PartOf=`. Grep across the whole tree shows **nothing** except `start.sh` and
the aliases references `balance_bot_web` — the Makefile and the build never
touched it. The unit also ran continuously for 13 h 31 m across a window
containing builds.

### `start.sh` is not in the service path

No unit calls it. `balance_bot.service` runs the binary directly. `start.sh`
is a **bench tool that replaces the systemd stack**: it stops
`balance_bot_server` + `balance_bot`, resets the RoboClaw, clears the e-stop,
starts its own `server.js` and `serve_web.py`, and runs the binary in the
foreground. It leaves all three units stopped on exit (the web one now
excepted). `rbots` is the recovery. Note `botss` is *status*, not restart —
that has caused confusion.

### Other corrections

- **Motor mode did not "change" to velocity.** It has been velocity for weeks.
  The 20 Hz CSV carries no motor config; I mistook first-sighting for a change.
- **`max_correction` 40/12/8 was bad advice and was reversed to 2.5.** Logs
  show 0% recovery past 10° and the body overshooting the command by up to
  2.11×. Past ±4° it is going down; past 6° it is out of bounds. The fence is
  12.5. Do not propose large correction ceilings.
- **A reported 4.5 s "control freeze" with motors pegged did not exist** — my
  step-trace script ran past the end of the file. Zero frozen stretches are
  actually present.
- **A settle metric counted a fallen bot as settled** (stopped encoder = zero
  error). Any settle statistic needs an upright gate.
- **The velocity estimator was tried and rejected** — see `HANDOFF.md` §4. Do
  not retry it blind.

## 8. Constants and gotchas that have bitten

- **`enc_pos` is left+right SUMMED.** mm/tick = πD/(2·PPR). Easy to be 2× out.
- **The wheel is a BaneBots 4-5/8" (~117 mm), not 155 mm.** Some code says 155.
- **Three inconsistent tick→distance constants exist in the tree.** Not yet
  reconciled.
- **20 Hz telemetry vs 100 Hz control loop** — the CSV aliases. Use the looplog
  when timing matters; it captures every tick.
- **`theta` comes from the fused DMP estimate, `theta_dot` from the raw gyro.**
  Two different sensors feeding two different arming gates.
- **M1 = RIGHT, M2 = LEFT.**
- **`set_both(0,0)` in velocity mode is a request to HOLD zero, not an absence
  of drive.** `motor_hal_coast()` sends `roboclaw_duty_m1m2(0,0)` for genuinely
  unpowered.
- **systemd:** `PartOf`/`BindsTo` propagate stop but not start;
  `Restart=on-failure` does not restart after a clean SIGTERM.
- **Position hold is not a PID.** No kp/ki/kd. It is zone-scheduled:
  `posCorr = enc_error / active_scale`, `velDamp = enc_velocity / vel_scale_stop`,
  `thetaAdj = −posCorr + posIterm − velDamp`, rate-limited by `max_angle_rate`,
  clamped by `max_correction`.
- **The 2 A bench supply is not a valid test rig.** It has been clipping every
  correction transient, so the bench plant and the LiPo plant are genuinely
  different systems — and on the LiPo nothing limits a runaway. This may be
  part of why the takeoff does not reproduce consistently.
